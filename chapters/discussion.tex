\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What Including Debate Reveals About Role-Playing Games}
\label{sec:rpg-insights}

The meeting of playtest and theoretical expectations gave birth to a handful of insights:

Firstly, on alibi. The framework currently treats alibi as a single concept appearing under two complementary glosses. \textcite{deterding2017alibis} names the \textit{justification-alibi}: the rationale an adult adopts as licence to play in the first place. \textcite{bowman2025transformative} names the \textit{performance-alibi}: the in-play permission to act, decide, and feel under cover of character. The Inside Out playtests show these are not parallel but coupled. The Rolling Out closing ritual, a framing device of the kind most LARPs use without difficulty, was reported as ``robotic'' in the first session and met with mumbling in the second. Yet the same participants spontaneously adopted ``Inner Voices'' as in-play address. Both moves were formally licensed by the same in-play permission. What discriminated them was justification: chanting felt decorative under a rationale that read play as ``learning argumentation,'' but natural where the rationale was ``inhabiting a character.'' The justification an adult brings to the table places a prior on the performance-alibis they will exercise once seated. The contribution to the framework is to treat the adult-justification surface not as a separate designable surface from the in-play one, but as a filter on it.

The second refinement concerns evaluator roles. Reform discourse around debate's value-convergence problem has long treated judges as neutral arbiters in need of better calibration. The role-playing-game framing identifies a different mechanism. Standard formats ask every judge to roleplay the same archetype, the average, unbiased person, and that is what produces drift. Conflating unbiased with lacking values procedurally teaches that certain values are a baseline and thus pulls people to adhere to them. Inside Out's playtests proved that its structural choices can directly prevent that. By prescribing for the Subject a clearly biased character and asking players to also voice their interpretative biases over that character's beliefs during the S1 speech, it led to players' realization of the need for tolerance towards others' beliefs and circumstantial decisions. This becomes thus a clean demonstration of the thesis's methodological claim: only treating the judge as a roleplayed character names the parameter that needed redesign, where the standard reform vocabulary of bias and calibration does not. 

This is further substantiated by the manner in which the apparent tension in the Subject role (emotional investment and evaluative neutrality) was proved to be easier resolved by leaning into roleplay in a \textit{steered} \parencite{montola2015steering, pohjola2015steering} fashion. This is a mirror of my and Seo's (2024) thesis that the cognitive overload of debate is only manageable by genuine character inhabiting. This process of steered roleplay proves thus to be employable in the pursuit of performance and with it towards memetic bleed, in itself a teachable capacity that can help teach other capacities (the long term cognitive abilities that form the transformative potential of debate).

The fourth refinement concerns where pressure comes from once external competition has been removed. The framework anticipated that removing win/loss outcomes would require a replacement source of competitive motivation, and the commendation architecture was designed to provide it. The playtests instead surfaced alongside them the alternative mechanism of social recognition one's performance. This mirrors the type of performance pressure found in LARP communities in which there is no formal performance metric. This raises questions on whether container safety is better reached by enforcing a culture of fake appraisals and hushed judgements, or one of open critique of players' performance, shaped constructively now that it is in the open.

All these lead not just to the pedantic overproof of debate as a roleplaying game. Debate is a special category of roleplaying games - it is evaluative, unavoidably somewhat conflictual and naturally more performance demanding than many roleplaying games. By stopping debating it is that and focusing on theorizing its inner workings, we discover further insights about other roleplaying games as well.

\subsection{Directions for Continued Study}
\label{sec:further-study}

\subsubsection{Progression Architecture}
\label{sec:progression}

The commendation system was received with genuine enthusiasm --- one evaluator described it as ``scratching the make-a-build itch'' --- but the same evaluators reported difficulty assigning commendations consistently, and one named the risk of ``farming'': stylised play optimised for commendation coverage rather than development. Both difficulties point to the same gap. The framework specifies what transformation \textit{is} --- bleed, identity work, memetic uptake --- but neither how a facilitator \textit{sees} it occurring in real time, nor how a progression structure can be designed so that optimising for its rewards is identical to the development the rewards are meant to register. Gamification and progressive-game-design literatures have developed substantial theory on exactly this alignment problem: reward loops, milestone structure, and the calibration of extrinsic incentives against the intrinsic capacities they are meant to cultivate. Integrating that work into transformative-game-design theory is a research direction the playtests sharpen but that the field would benefit from regardless. The gap is general; the commendation system is one instance of it.

\subsubsection{Governance as a Designable Surface}
\label{sec:governance}

The most consequential observation in the second debrief was the participants' own reconstruction of how speedtalking became British Parliamentary's meta and then contaminated World Schools after the judge pools merged. Their account was not about rules. As the founding university teachers disengaged from BP, rule interpretation fell to whoever held social power in the circuit, and that group systematically privileged the skill hardest to master because that was what sustained their standing. A new format dropped into that community inherits the same governance pathology regardless of its ruleset. \textcite{bowman2025transformative}'s ``community support'' category does not reach this. Governance --- how rules are interpreted, how evaluators are trained, who authors motions, whose voice sets the meta's drift --- is a designable surface above the format, and arguably more consequential for long-run behaviour than anything in the format specification. The community-management literature developed for online and in-person practice communities has tools transformative-game-design has not yet integrated. The applicability is general: any transformative format whose long-run behaviour depends on a sustaining community --- LARP scenes, educational role-play programmes, simulation networks --- inherits the same surface.

\subsubsection{Sustained Peer-Recognition Pressure}
\label{sec:peer-recognition-study}

Section~\ref{sec:rpg-insights} named peer recognition as a third pressure category in transformative formats. Doing so opens questions the playtests cannot answer. How does peer-recognition pressure scale beyond the bounded session, where every participant can attend to every other? How does it interact with the progression architecture studied in Section~\ref{sec:progression}? What failure modes does it carry: clique formation, the in-group bias a small group compounds, the same homogenising drift that a unitary evaluator role produces in standard debate? These are not debate-specific questions. They apply to any transformative format that has chosen to remove external competition and must therefore source motivational intensity elsewhere --- a design choice many such formats make, and few have studied past first-order container claims.

\subsubsection{Modularity as a Research Programme}
\label{sec:modularity}

The framework tends to treat transformative formats as integrated wholes whose components reinforce one another. Inside Out is also that, but it is additionally decomposable. Five of its design moves can be transplanted into an existing format independently of the others: the commendation system; the split between an in-debate Interlocutor whose performance is evaluated and an external Evaluator whose is not; three teams rather than two; a judge who roleplays a specific named character; and teams prescribed values but granted creative freedom over the solutions that express them. Each is a candidate for single-mechanism transplantation, which invites a research methodology in which transformative mechanisms are studied as transplantable units rather than as constituents of holistic experiences. The lens generalises beyond Inside Out. Any transformative format proposed in the literature can be analysed for which of its components are load-bearing mechanisms and which are consequences of integration. Mainstream game-design analysis already treats mechanic-level contributions in this way; importing the habit into transformative-game-design study would sharpen the field's empirical purchase.

\bigskip

The two arguments compose a single contribution. From the encounter between debate and transformative-game-design theory, the framework gains both refined constructs --- the coupling of alibi senses, the design status of evaluator roles, the importability of steering, peer recognition as a pressure category --- and a research programme that the next iteration of work, on Inside Out or elsewhere, can now pursue.
