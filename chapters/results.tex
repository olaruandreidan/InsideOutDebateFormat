\section{RESULTS}
\label{sec:results}

The research question breaks into two sub-questions. First: can a research method of applying transformative game design theory to this problem domain be demonstrated? Second: does that research method yield expected results? Each is addressed in turn.

\subsection{Demonstrating the Method: The Design Process as Contribution}

The first sub-question is answered by the thesis itself. The systematic progression from theoretical analysis through iterative design to empirical testing constitutes a worked example of operationalizing transformative game design theory in debate. The reasoning connecting each design iteration to observed player behavior is documented in Annexes A, B, and G; whether or not the resulting format succeeds transformatively, the method has been retroactively collaged as narratively explicit and academically repeatable.

\subsection{Evaluating the Results: Evidence from Playtests}

The second sub-question concerns whether the designed format shows signs of achieving its intended effects. Results were analyzed using the clue-taxonomy method described in Section~\ref{sec:methods}; the complete taxonomy is in Annex~I. Each of the five evaluation fields corresponds to a prediction made in Section~\ref{sec:design} on the basis of the theoretical framework, and each is assessed below as a constellation of clues whose configuration points toward an answer.

\subsubsection{Field 1: Engagement, Appeal, and Long-Term Viability}

Field~1 asks whether the format generates genuine voluntary engagement; without it, the transformational container cannot form and bleed cannot occur. The design was predicted to generate this engagement by replacing status-competition with imaginative investment, with onboarding cost predicted as real but not prohibitive. The prediction was \textit{confirmed}: imaginative investment manifested in emergent behavior the ruleset did not script, and the differential engagement pattern across participant profiles is consistent with design intent.

The primary signal is spontaneous adoption of ``inner voices'' as in-play address, a term absent from the ruleset and generated organically during play. Two converging supports tighten the picture: animated post-session conversations extending beyond the structured debrief in both sessions, and players experimenting with uninstructed and unrewarded creative choices (accents, performance styles, personal props). Self-reports add a third strand: the format seen as ``surprisingly liberating,'' the Huddle phase ``very very fun,'' and similar positive characterizations, all offered unsolicited across both sessions.

The differential engagement pattern is itself evidence that emancipatory bleed \parencite{kemper2017battle, kemper2020wyrding} is operating as theorized. In the sessions observed, women and debaters with less successful traditional-format track records expressed consistent preference, while three of the fifteen participants across both sessions (all competitive men with strong traditional-format track records) expressed reservations, one describing the format as ``anticlimactic'' and missing the ``ego-boosting'' experience of decisive victory. The format was not designed to serve the highly competitive profile that traditional formats already serve; its appeal to voices suppressed by traditional formats is what the framework predicts when status-competition is replaced by imaginative investment under a strengthened alibi \parencite{bowman2021magic, deterding2017alibis}.

Two qualifications: the commendation system produced a subtler negative clue, with Evaluators in both sessions finding commendation assignment cognitively overwhelming at small informal scale. The system was designed for tournament use where Evaluators have prior familiarity with the badge list; the difficulty observed here is a calibration issue specific to off-tournament context, not a structural failure. The 40-minute onboarding brief confirms a real learning curve, while both sessions producing engaged play within a single session confirms the cost is onboarding, not fundamental inaccessibility. The Rolling Out ritual recurred as a disengagement signal across both sessions (discussed in Field~3).

\subsubsection{Field 2: Productive Pressure Mechanisms}

Field~2 asks whether productive intellectual pressure persists after removal of win/loss outcomes. The prediction was that pressure would be redirected rather than removed, with the commendation architecture replacing victory as the evaluative anchor. The prediction was \textit{confirmed in outcome with an informative deviation in mechanism}: pressure persisted at high levels across both sessions, but the operative driver was an emergent social-recognition dynamic rather than the formal commendation system the design predicted.

On the outcome side (was pressure preserved?), the first session produced elaborate, nuanced solutions with careful value-priority arguments and passionate speeches, and the second produced contextually rich, roleplay-oriented speeches with uninstructed worldbuilding detail. The risk that emotional expressiveness might displace analytical rigor did not materialize: practical reasoning and value hierarchies remained present alongside the emotional engagement. A first-session self-report (``architecting solutions'' as ``very complex and interesting strategically'') aligns with the behavioral picture. A novel positive clue from the second session adds force: participants spontaneously initiated a group conversation about how to diminish another Inner Voice's contribution without being dismissive, a growth discussion standard debate does not produce because the skill is not rewarded there. The absence of apathy in both sessions, with no participant reducing effort in the win/loss vacuum, weighs as supporting evidence.

On the mechanism side, the operative driver was not the commendation architecture but a social one: participants wanted recognition from fellow Inner Voices and from the Subject for the quality and resonance of their contributions. The commendation system provided the evaluative frame, but peer recognition motivated effort. This is an informative deviation rather than a failure: it refines the procedural-rhetoric account \parencite{bogost2007persuasive} by showing that the operative rule can be a social rule emergent from the formal structure rather than the formal scoring rule itself, and it converges with the Field~4 finding that informal social reward did the work of formal commendation when the latter was unreliable.

The main qualification concerns the Subject role's scaffolding gap. In the first playtest, the Subject described not knowing ``what to say,'' uncertain how to hold emotional investment and evaluative neutrality at once; this was addressed in the second iteration's instructions. A second scaffolding issue then emerged in the second session, where the Subject ran out of prepared Huddle questions before the allotted time. That session also offered a partial resolution: the Subject who navigated the role most successfully was the one who leaned into roleplay rather than maintaining analytical detachment. Inhabiting the character, responding emotionally, and asking from genuine curiosity resolved the cognitive tension between roleplay and evaluation, consistent with the broader argument in Section~\ref{sec:background} that debate is fundamentally a form of role-playing.

\subsubsection{Field 3: Growth Direction -- Useful Skills and Perspectives}

Field~3 asks whether the format produces growth in the spectrum of useful skills documented in Section~\ref{sec:background} as the basis of debate's transformative reputation: critical thinking, communication and public-speaking proficiency, and empowerment of voices that traditional formats suppress. The prediction was that the format would preserve standard debate's analytical gains while extending them along axes traditional formats develop weakly: persuasion addressed to a sympathetic non-expert listener and accessibility to participants without prior debate training. The prediction was \textit{strongly confirmed}.

Critical thinking and analytical rigor were preserved, not displaced by the format's emotional register. Both sessions produced elaborate, nuanced solutions; participants navigated value hierarchies and inter-value tensions without prompting; practical reasoning sat alongside roleplay rather than being crowded out. First-session self-reports of ``architecting solutions'' as ``very complex and interesting strategically'' converge with the behavioural picture. Further self-reports articulating learning ``to think about the context of decisions,'' to be ``more human and more honest about human nature,'' and to recognise that ``sometimes decisions are personal, not universal'' indicate the analytical frame did not narrow but expanded toward contextually grounded reasoning.

Communication and public-speaking proficiency---the dimension on which traditional formats are most often critiqued---developed along axes traditional debate develops weakly. Players in the second session adopted second-person address, direct eye contact with the Subject, and contextual worldbuilding from the opening of the debate, all uninstructed: audience adaptation became the primary rhetorical mode rather than a secondary consideration. Expressive range broadened in unscripted directions: accents, performance styles, a soundtrack played behind a Subject speech, worldbuilt characters with emotional stakes. Participants explicitly identified this dimension, noting that the format develops ``conversational skills and empathy without necessarily having this educational background.''

That formulation also names the empowerment dimension. Voices the traditional formats suppress---in this sample, women and debaters with less successful traditional-format track records---consistently expressed preference for the format, and Field~1's differential engagement pattern reads here as voice development, not only retention. Across both sessions, play diverged from standard-debate conventions speech by speech, the novel-over-continuity weighting principle reading these adjustments as effects of the format rather than imported habit. The two predicted negative clues---unhelpful play rewarded and hollow performative emotionality---did not manifest in either session.

The Rolling Out ritual is the most significant design issue discovered here: the in-round skills become genuinely transferable only if the transformational container holds through closing, and a closing ritual that breaks immersion truncates that arc. The redesigned version in the third iteration (Annex~H) addresses this directly but remains untested in this thesis cycle.

\subsubsection{Field 4: Community Dynamics and Reduced Toxicity}

Field~4 asks whether removing win/loss outcomes and reshaping the evaluative structure toward commendation reduces the adversarial dynamics that drive community attrition and harm. The prediction was that status derived from demonstrated growth, rather than from competitive victory, would dismantle the dynamics that erode the transformational container. The prediction was \textit{strongly confirmed}: the constellation of behavioral norms, multi-source self-reports, and meaningful absences across two independent sessions shows the strongest cross-clue convergence in the dataset.

The clearest behavioral signal is an emergent norm against adversarial rebuttal: in the second playtest, participants spontaneously articulated that ``outright attacking others' points feels wrong, disrespectful, and unnecessary''---a norm absent from the ruleset, arising from the format's structural logic. This is procedural rhetoric in the precise sense \textcite{bogost2007persuasive} theorizes: rebuttal carries no competitive benefit while contextual contribution does, inverting the rhetorical incentive structure of standard debate. Reduced anxiety converges from another angle: across both sessions, participants independently reported the format as ``much much less anxiety-inducing,'' described a ``feeling of salvation regardless of result,'' and noted that ``everyone distinguished himself by his own style''---unsolicited, multi-source reports across two independent groups, consistent with the transformational container forming as theorized \parencite{bowman2021magic, bowman2025transformative}.

Brave, unconventional play forms a third strand: vulnerable, emotionally intimate approaches in the first session and roleplay-heavy delivery, soundtrack-opened Subject speeches, and worldbuilt characters with emotional stakes in the second drew immediate gestural reward (laughing, nodding, leaning forward). This experimentation was possible because the Inner Voices framing operates as Deterding's \parencite{deterding2017alibis} alibi, distancing performer from position and removing the identity threat that constrains risk-taking in win/loss formats. The fourth direction is what was \textit{not} observed: none of the three predicted negative clues (distress, arrogant or unempathetic behavior, praise of such behavior) manifested across either session, despite participants being experienced competitive debaters with established adversarial habits and despite the second session's moments of genuine emotional intensity (see Field~5).

Two qualifications narrow the result without weakening it. The anxiety reduction may carry a novelty-artifact component: with no established status, less social capital is staked on performance, and a portion of the reduction may reflect that initial condition rather than a structural feature that would persist in a mature Inside Out circuit. The qualification is one of durability under maturation, not of the structural effect demonstrated within the playtest window. The commendation system's calibration immaturity at small scale, noted in Field~1, recurs here in a more theoretically revealing form: where the formal commendation channel was unreliable, the room's gestural and verbal social reward did the same evaluative work---a second instance of the social-recognition mechanism named in Field~2.

\subsubsection{Field 5: Epistemic Humility}

Field~5 tests the deepest predicted transformation: that the Inner Voices framing, the S0 bias disclosure, the absence of a winner, and the multi-perspective structure produce a shift in how participants hold their own beliefs as an emergent consequence of inhabiting the format's structural logic. The mechanism is memetic bleed \parencite{hugaas2024bleed, leonard2018bleed} steering toward value pluralism rather than convergence. The prediction was \textit{confirmed at proof-of-concept level}: the constellation centers on the single highest-strength clue in the dataset and is supported by behavioral, articulated, and absent-as-evidence strands.

The focal observation is an unplanned bleed event in the second session: a participant disclosed privately to the researcher that the scenario in play resonated with a real-life situation they were facing that same day. This is the highest-strength clue in the dataset because three weighting principles align: it is behavioral rather than self-report, it is private rather than performed for the researcher, and it instantiates the transformational mechanism directly, with the format's content crossing the play/life boundary as a memetic resource for an actual decision \parencite{hugaas2024bleed}. One event in one session is not generalizable, but as proof-of-concept evidence it shows the mechanism functioning exactly as theorized.

Three in-play correlates fill out the picture. Synthesis orientation emerged across both rounds of Inner Voices speeches, appearing in the first round and intensifying in the second---spontaneous drift toward convergence despite no rule incentivizing it, transcript-confirmed and novel, the clearest behavioral indicator that the format's structural logic orients participants toward integration rather than dominance. Players in the second session organically renamed themselves ``Inner Voices'' during play, positioning themselves as parts of a shared internal deliberation rather than as opposing sides (subsequently formalized in the third iteration). The second-session debrief produced a meta-confirmation: participants independently identified that the S0 institutionalizes surfacing personal biases openly before argumentation, reframing bias as perspective rather than contamination---an analysis they arrived at without prompting, which is itself evidence the mechanism functioned: the format made its own epistemic logic visible to its players. First-session self-reports converge from another angle: one participant articulated the attitudinal shift unprompted (``every team says valuable stuff and it is important more to see what is important to us''), and others observed that arguing someone is ``morally wrong'' for a personal decision feels ``a bit absurd'' in this format. The strands together trace one phenomenon: value pluralism replacing value convergence as the format's procedural lesson \parencite{bogost2007persuasive}.

Three qualifications limit what can be claimed. The bleed event is a single observation and cannot demonstrate persistence. Both sessions used small samples of experienced debaters; whether the same configuration produces the same effect at scale is what the next design cycle would test. No longitudinal follow-up was conducted; the durability of the shift beyond the playtest window is unestablished. By contrast, the absence of unequivocal condemnation of positions or belief-holders, or praise of such condemnation, holds across both sessions despite motions involving genuine ethical weight and participants practiced in adversarial argumentation, strengthening confidence beyond what the positive clues alone would justify.

\subsection{Proof of Concept Assessment}

The five fields together compose a single causal account rather than five independent verdicts: each later field's confirmation rests on the earlier field's effect being present.

Two cross-cutting framework concepts unify the account. Procedural rhetoric \parencite{bogost2007persuasive} appears in Fields~3, 4, and 5: a rule structure that rewards contextual contribution but not rebuttal produces perspective-taking, collaborative framing, synthesis, and the explicit norm against adversarial rebuttal. Bleed mechanisms map onto the remaining fields: emancipatory bleed \parencite{kemper2017battle, kemper2020wyrding} for Field~1's reach to suppressed voices, procedural bleed for Field~3's transferable skills, memetic bleed \parencite{hugaas2024bleed} for Field~5's value-pluralism shift. Each concept's predictions hold across its fields, supporting design-account coherence rather than field-specific post-hoc rationalization.

 % The most interesting design finding is a refinement of that account itself. Field~2's mechanism deviation (social recognition driving pressure rather than commendations) and Field~4's converging observation (informal social reward doing the work of formal commendation when calibration was unreliable) together suggest that rule-structure shapes behavior, but the operative rules need not be the formal scoring rules: they can be social-recognition rules that emerge from the formal structure. 

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations bound what these results can claim. First, the original design envisioned approximately four iterative playtest sessions; two were completed within the thesis timeline. Both yielded substantive observational and debrief data sufficient for proof-of-concept assessment, but the reduced iteration count narrows the breadth of design refinement reflected here. Second, two recurring design gaps---Subject role scaffolding (Field~2) and the Rolling Out ritual's immersion-breaking effect (Field~3)---were partially addressed across iterations but remain open questions; both should be prioritized in the next design cycle. Third, both playtest groups consisted of experienced debaters already familiar with competitive formats; the format's performance with novice participants remains untested. The methodological constraints articulated in Section~\ref{sec:methods} continue to bound inference: the constellation across the five fields supports a coherent design account but cannot demonstrate persistence, superiority, or generalizability.
