Some thoughts that have surfaced: 

On non-binarism, it is very interesting how the removal of clear cut binary logic (no two teams, no clear winner and loser) had a somewhat feminist yet gender-binary confirming effect: all ppl that were somewhat dissapointed in the design were men, all women reported strongly how much less anxious this made them feel. This in particular does raise interesting questions for other deliberative games, on how they can be designed with potential gender differential responses in mind, and how there is much to still investigate on this and how this is a dimension of design and research that at least I have yet to address much.

On how much I do not actually investigate and take for granted as known facts on how a facilitator ought to act, whether for this game or for other games or for work meetings and how in my mind this ought to probably apply to other fields as well such as therapy, but that I have yet to read about this much and how such a game can also be a good platform to check how different instructions for the facilitator do help. 

On my very clear plans for this game: I believe that the correct path for making the most out of this research is to harness it as a pilot research and use it to apply to various Erasmus and Horizon grants. I plan on doing this in consortiums between the game-dev company I have been running for many years now (Critique Gaming) and other educational institutions (and this Masters at Uppsala University had also as a purpose furthering ties with the faculty and the department there), and also educational institutions operating in the debate world (IDEA and ARDOR especially) and maybe even governmental authorities (the Romanian Ministry of Education) in order to properly fund the development of a robust process for using transformative game design to refine the use of debate as an educational and transformative tool. In particular I want to fund a long term project, in which together with other game designers, scholars, NGO executives and debate experts we build an entire circuit of debate events and recurrent debate-based educational processes, based on the existing infrastructure of debate clubs and debate competitions. This would feature a pipeline for clearly collecting data both quantitatively and qualitatively, iterating actively on the design of the debate formats we are to experiment with, measuring their transformative impact. This would be a platform for writing a robust PhD thesis on the same topic.
